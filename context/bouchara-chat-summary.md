## 1. How the case was chosen
The chat considered four struggling companies, each paired with a digital model that already works elsewhere:
- Envibus, paired with Jelbi (a combined mobility app)
- Gibert Jeune, paired with Bookshop.org
- André, paired with a resale marketplace
- **Bouchara**

Bouchara was chosen because of its new owner. AA Investments (Samir Goshayeshi) also owns **BazarChic** (a flash-sale site with a "Maison" home category and a 7am daily email), Wethenew, Smallable and L'Exception. It has a repeatable way of rescuing distressed French retail brands through digital channels.

## 2. Company facts
- Founded in Marseille in 1899. The retailer was previously called Eurodif and moved fully to home textiles and décor (bedding, curtains, fabric by the metre, tableware) under the Bouchara name from 2017.
- Went into receivership in January 2026, then liquidation. At that point it had 52 stores, which made **95% of sales, against 5% online**.
- On 11 May 2026 the Paris court approved a takeover by AA Investments. It kept **25 of the 52 stores and 184 of the 542 jobs**. The unions (FO, CGT) criticised the terms.
- The 25 stores are in: Rennes, Alençon, Amiens, Bordeaux, Brest, Brive-la-Gaillarde, Caen, Chalon-sur-Saône, Dijon, Épernay, Évreux, La Rochelle, Le Havre, Limoges, Lorient, Lyon, Marseille, Nantes, Nice, Orléans, Rouen, Saint-Étienne, Tours, Vannes and Versailles. None is in central Paris; the footprint is provincial.

## 3. Evidence behind the hybrid model
- **Online share of European furniture sales:** about 19% (2021), rising to about 28% (2026), so more than 70% is still bought offline. The UK is at about 34% and Southern Europe at about 21% (CSIL, Statista).
- **Shoppers still want stores:** surveys show 58–77% prefer buying furniture in store, and **44% "showroom"** (look in store, buy online). Sofas, bedding and textiles are the categories where this happens most. *These are US and UK trade surveys.*
- **France-specific (Showroomprivé × NellyRodi, June 2024, n=1,643, weighted):**
  - 93% of French home-décor buyers use both online and physical channels.
  - 63% buy through discount or flash-sale sites.
  - 86% say price is central to their decision.
  - This study was commissioned by Showroomprivé, so it isn't independent.
- **IPEA / Institut de la Maison** (the sector's own research institute):
  - The French furniture market was worth €13.8bn in 2025 (-1.8%).
  - Online sales are growing in bedding and upholstered furniture.
  - 25.3% of online buyers are 60 or older.
- **The low end of the market is taken by imports:** French makers are moving upmarket (BusinessCoot, January 2025; Xerfi says the same).
- **Omnichannel customers are worth more:**
  - HBR study (46,000 shoppers): multichannel customers spend 4% more per store visit and 10% more online, and make 23% more repeat visits.
  - Adyen: +15% spend.
  - Doppio/Incisiv: 1.5× monthly spend and 3× more loyal.
  - Academic backing: Verhoef, Kannan & Inman (2015), *Journal of Retailing*.
  - Further support from McKinsey, BCG and Deloitte (who note pure digital-native brands moving into physical retail).
- **Useful cases:**
  - Made.com went online-only and collapsed in 2022.
  - Maisons du Monde is about 50% online with 349 stores; it is the benchmark.
  - Burrow saw online conversions double in New York after opening a showroom; its store converts at 22%.
  - Warby Parker's omnichannel customers have 65% higher lifetime value.
  - Allbirds and Interior Define show that stores need cost discipline to pay off.
- **Configurator precedents:** Sven/Svensson (Nordic, B2B curtains), JABS Group, Mobibam (Paris) and Sophia Home Decoration.

**Source-quality rule the user set:** prefer academic papers, big consultancies and research institutes (IPEA, CSIL, Xerfi, INSEE). Label brand-commissioned surveys (Vivastreet, Showroomprivé) as such, and don't cite blogs.

## 4. Customers
**B2C, core buyer**
- Age 40–65, in the 25 store cities. The strongest territory is the mid-sized and small towns, where IKEA and Maisons du Monde have no store.
- Above-average income.
- Decisions are usually led by women (Vivastreet: 60% vs 29%).
- Shops in the showroom first.

**B2C, growth buyer**
- Age 25–44, already on BazarChic, sensitive to price, finds products online first.

**B2B**
- About 3,500 artisan upholsterer businesses (ONISEP).
- Interior decorators are an unregulated profession, so there is no official count. They charge about €80–150 an hour.
- These buyers value speed and accurate fabric calculations.

**Market-size funnel** (a rough estimate)
| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| Catchment population | about 8.6M |
| Households (÷2.2) | about 3.9M |

| Spend >€500/yr (39%) | **about 490k households** (broad target) |
| Narrow target if limited to homeowners | about 122k |

## 5. How the concept developed
1. **First idea:** use the 25 stores as showrooms, add an online configurator for made-to-measure curtains and fabric, and sell overstock through BazarChic. Plan it in phases: 2026 stabilise, 2027 launch the configurator, 2028 and later add a trade channel.
2. **Mission:** "Make custom-made French home textiles as easy to buy as ready-made ones, without losing physical trust."
3. **Customer journey maps:**
   - A cold-customer version and a warm-customer version (someone who already knows Bouchara uses the AI room preview, touches the fabric in store, gives measurements and waits for the custom order).
   - A branded 5-phase version: Discovery, Inspire, Experience, Fulfill, Retain.
   - A PowerPoint in the Columbia Road style, with 8 rows and an experience curve scoring 3, 4, 3, 2, 4 across the stages.
   - **Main pain points:**
     - People can't judge fabric from a photo.
     - They worry about measuring wrong.
     - Only 25 towns have a store.
     - The AI preview may not match the real product.
     - Custom orders take longer.
     - There's no easy way to return a custom item.
4. **Growth, pricing and blueprint (SCQA method):**
   - **Growth means adoption, not acquisition.** Move the existing customer base along the adoption curve: Year 1 early adopters, Year 2 crossing the chasm, Year 3 the late majority. The metric is the **percentage of known customers using a digital feature**.
   - **Pricing: one price everywhere** (the Zara parity model). The only exception is **BazarChic, which may discount ready-made or overstock items, never custom orders**. This is hybrid pricing, in contrast to Uniqlo's store-only deals. B2B would be a paid trade account (recurring income).
   - **The Digital Business Blueprint** was filled in: capabilities, services, networks, assets, value propositions, customer groups, funding and value measures (digital adoption %, online share moving towards 50%, repeat purchases and referrals, on-time delivery).
   - **Partner-review warning:** there is no single customer ID linking the showroom, the app and BazarChic, so adoption can't be measured yet.
5. **Proposition package** (the course's five categories):
   - Magnet: a free box of fabric samples
   - Starter: BazarChic flash sales
   - Core: the made-to-measure configurator
   - Premium: the AI room preview plus in-store styling advice
   - Bespoke: a B2B trade account
6. **Step back, in plain terms:** the product is a digital layer that lets people see, measure and order from home, then finish in the showroom. The app has five jobs:
   - AI preview
   - measuring guide
   - ordering swatches
   - placing and tracking the order
   - booking a showroom visit

   Recommendation: start with a **mobile web tool rather than a native app**, because the customer base is older.

## 6. Where the document stands now ("Bouchara Final")
The concept has changed to **"Bouchara Maison"**:
- Customers save a room project and get coordinated product suggestions within a budget.
- They preview the room with AI, then finish with advice in the showroom.
- Made-to-measure is an optional extra, offered where suppliers support it.
- BazarChic is "subject to commercial agreement", not guaranteed.

The document adds PESTEL, SWOT, a competitor gap table (IKEA Kreativ, Heytens, Madura, Maisons du Monde), six targets up to 2029, and a pilot to test demand.
