# Framework templates

Each framework below has: its origin, when to use it, a fill-in template, and a short example. Replace the placeholders with the user's real material. Never invent facts.

## Contents
1. StoryBrand (Donald Miller)
2. Golden Circle (Simon Sinek)
3. But / Therefore (Trey Parker & Matt Stone)
4. Pixar story spine (Kenn Adams / Emma Coats)
5. Challenger Sale choreography (Dixon & Adamson)

7. Hero's Journey (Joseph Campbell, simplified by Christopher Vogler)
8. In Medias Res
9. Data storytelling
10. Bonus micro-structures: Before-After-Bridge, PAS, STAR, "What? So what? Now what?"

## 1. StoryBrand (SB7)
**Use for** websites, proposals, sales emails and value propositions.
```
CHARACTER   – [Customer] wants [specific desire].
PROBLEM     – External: [tangible issue]. Internal: [how it makes them feel]. Philosophical: [why it's wrong that they should have to deal with this].
GUIDE       – We understand ([empathy line]) and we've done it before ([authority: proof, numbers, logos]).
PLAN        – 1. [step] 2. [step] 3. [step]
CALL TO ACTION – Direct: [book a call]. Transitional: [download / free diagnostic].
FAILURE     – Without it: [cost of inaction].
SUCCESS     – With it: [vivid picture of the transformed state].
```
*Example:* "Operations leaders want to scale without adding headcount. But every new client means more manual handoffs, and your best people burn out on busywork. That shouldn't be the price of growth. We've redesigned operations for 40 mid-sized firms…"

## 2. Golden Circle
**Use for** vision, leadership messages, pitch openers and employer branding.
```
WHY  – The belief or purpose: what we believe about the world. Don't describe the product here.
HOW  – The distinctive approach or principles that bring the belief to life.
WHAT – The concrete offer or proof.
```
Test: the Why section should still make sense if you removed the product name.

## 3. But / Therefore
**Use for** case studies, status updates and any narrative that currently reads "and then… and then…".
Rule: replace every "and then" with **BUT** (conflict) or **THEREFORE** (consequence).
```
[Starting fact].
BUT [complication / obstacle].
THEREFORE [action we took].
BUT [new problem or surprise].
THEREFORE [adaptation → result].
```
*Example:* "The bank launched a new app. BUT digital onboarding took 11 days. THEREFORE we mapped the journey with 20 customers. BUT the bottleneck turned out to be identity checks, not the app. THEREFORE we redesigned the KYC step, and onboarding now takes 1 day."

## 4. Pixar story spine
**Use for** origin stories, change management, transformation narratives and team talks.
```
Once upon a time, [status quo].
Every day, [routine / how things worked].
Until one day, [inciting incident / disruption].
Because of that, [consequence 1].
Because of that, [consequence 2].
Until finally, [climax / decision / breakthrough].
And ever since then, [new normal + lesson].
```
A useful Pixar rule (from Emma Coats' 22 rules): give the character an opinion and let them struggle. People admire a character for trying more than for succeeding.

## 5. Challenger Sale choreography
**Use for** complex B2B sales, insight-led pitches, and getting clients to rethink the problem.
```
1. WARMER           – Show you understand their world: [peer challenges, validated].
2. REFRAME          – "What most [role]s miss is…" [surprising insight that links to your unique strength].
3. RATIONAL DROWNING – Data showing the hidden cost: [numbers, benchmarks].
4. EMOTIONAL IMPACT – "Companies like yours…" [a story of someone who felt this pain].
5. A NEW WAY        – The better approach, described without naming your product yet.
6. YOUR SOLUTION    – Why you are uniquely able to deliver the new way. Then take control: propose a concrete next step.
```

## 6. Minto Pyramid & SCQA
**Use for** executive briefings, recommendations, board papers and consulting emails.
```
SITUATION    – Uncontroversial context the audience already agrees with.
COMPLICATION – What changed or what is wrong (the tension).
QUESTION     – The question this raises (often left implicit).
ANSWER       – The governing thought or recommendation, stated FIRST in the document.
  ├─ Key line 1 (reason / step) – evidence
  ├─ Key line 2 – evidence
  └─ Key line 3 – evidence
NEXT STEP / ASK – the decision needed, by whom, by when.
```
Rules:
- Supporting points must be MECE (no overlaps, no gaps).
- Group them either inductively (grouped reasons) or deductively (a chain of logic), not both.
- Every heading should state a message, not just name a topic.
- For long documents, use the `pyramid-storyline` skill.

## 7. Hero's Journey (for business)
**Use for** personal stories, keynotes, customer transformation stories and founder stories.
```
ORDINARY WORLD – Who the hero is and what normal looks like.
CALL           – The problem or opportunity that forces change.
REFUSAL/DOUBT  – Why it was hard or risky (this makes the story believable).
MENTOR/GUIDE   – The insight, person or tool that helped (in a customer story, this is often you).
TRIALS         – 1–2 specific struggles, with concrete detail.
TRANSFORMATION – What changed, in the hero and in the results.
RETURN         – The lesson the audience can take and use.
```
In a customer story, the customer is the hero and the firm is the mentor. Don't reverse these roles.

## 8. In Medias Res
**Use for** openings, social posts, crisis communication and speeches.
```
HOOK   – Drop into the most intense moment: "At 2:14 a.m., the payment system went down for 3 million customers."
REWIND – "To understand how we got there, go back six months…"
BUILD  – The key events leading up to the peak.
RESOLVE – What happened next.
LESSON – The takeaway and the call to action.
```

## 9. Data storytelling
**Use for** investor updates, performance reviews, analytics readouts and dashboards.
```
HEADLINE INSIGHT – One sentence that says what the data means, not what it is. ("Enterprise is becoming our growth engine.")
CONTEXT          – A baseline, trend or benchmark that makes the numbers meaningful (vs last year, vs plan, vs peers).
TURNING POINT    – The 1–2 data points that show the change. Put numbers in human terms ("churn fell from 6.1% to 4.2%, which means 1 in 50 fewer customers leave every quarter").
CAUSE            – Why it happened: evidence, not guesses.
IMPLICATION      – So what for the business?
ASK / NEXT       – The decision or support needed.
```
Rules:
- Use at most 3 numbers per point.
- Round numbers sensibly.
- Compare every number to something.
- Use one chart per message and give it an action title (see the `chart-to-message-mapper` skill).

## 10. Micro-structures (for short pieces)
- **Before–After–Bridge:** the world now → the world after → how to get there (you).
- **PAS (Problem–Agitate–Solve):** name the pain → show what it costs → offer the fix.
- **STAR (Situation–Task–Action–Result):** for interviews, credentials and short case examples.
- **What? So what? Now what?:** for quick updates and reflections.
- **Rule of three:** three beats, three examples or three benefits. People remember groups of three.
