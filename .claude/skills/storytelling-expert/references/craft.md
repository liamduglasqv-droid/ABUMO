# Craft rules: the editing pass

Apply these after drafting in the chosen framework. They are drawn from widely taught storytelling practice: Pixar's rules, the Heath brothers' *Made to Stick*, Nancy Duarte's *Resonate*, and the Minto Pyramid and SCQA method used in consulting.

## Checklist
1. **Hook in the first line.** Open with tension, a surprising fact, a vivid moment or a question. Cut warm-up lines such as "In today's fast-paced world…"
2. **One clear hero.** Name one hero, ideally a specific person or a customer type, not "stakeholders".
3. **Stakes.** Say what is at risk if nothing changes: money, time, reputation, people.
4. **Conflict, the "but".** A story without an obstacle is just a report. Make the tension explicit.
5. **Concrete over abstract.** Use a named moment, a place, a number or a quote *(from the user's material only)*. "Onboarding took 11 days" beats "onboarding was slow".
6. **Show, don't tell.** Describe what happened and let the reader draw the conclusion ("the CFO closed her laptop") instead of labelling it ("the CFO was frustrated").
7. **Humanise the data.** Turn every key number into a comparison, a unit people can picture, or its impact on a person.
8. **Contrast.** Move between what is and what could be (Duarte). The gap between them creates energy.
9. **One message.** Remove every sentence that doesn't serve the core message, even if it's true and interesting.
10. **Transformation.** The ending should show change: in the hero, in the numbers, or in what becomes possible.
11. **Clear call to action.** Say exactly what should happen next, who does it and by when.
12. **Rhythm.** Mix short and long sentences. Use a one-line paragraph for emphasis, and use it sparingly.
13. **Plain words.** Cut jargon, buzzwords and hedging. Prefer active voice with a named actor.
14. **Credibility.** Where the audience is skeptical, bring the proof in early: data, a client name (only if it's allowed), or your track record.

## The SUCCESs test (Made to Stick)
Ask whether the message is:
- **S**imple: is there one core idea?
- **U**nexpected: does something surprise the audience?
- **C**oncrete: can they picture it?
- **C**redible: is there proof?
- **E**motional: will they care?
- a **S**tory: is there a character, conflict and change?

## Adapting to the audience
| Audience | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| C-suite / board | Answer first, money and risk, a decision to make, under 1 minute to get the point |
| Investors | Market size and trajectory, why now, why us, traction as a story arc |
| Clients / buyers | Their world, their pain, their hero moment; you are the guide |
| Employees in change | Why change, what stays the same, what's in it for them, what to do on Monday |
| Social media / public | Hook in 1–2 lines, one idea, a personal voice, short paragraphs |

## Length guides
| Format | Length |
|---|---|
| Elevator pitch | 30–60 seconds, about 75–150 words |
| LinkedIn post | 150–300 words, with a hook before the "see more" cut-off |
| Executive email | 5–10 lines above the fold, with the answer in the first 2 |
| Case study | 300–600 words |
| Keynote story | 2–4 minutes per story |

## Common failures to fix
- Starting with the company history instead of the customer's problem.
- Listing features instead of showing a transformation.
- Several heroes and several messages.
- Data dumps with no headline insight.
- A happy ending with no struggle, which makes the story unbelievable.
- An ending with no ask.
