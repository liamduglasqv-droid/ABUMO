# Diagnostics for starting an engagement

Stratability's online *Strategy Journey Analyzer* (strategyjourney.com/analyzer-tool) plays this role officially. This file is a self-contained alternative for use in a chat.

## 1. Intake questions (ask only for what is missing)
1. Organisation: industry, size, geography, stage (start-up, scale-up, mature, turnaround).
2. Trigger: why now? What happens if nothing is done?
3. The user's role and mandate: board, executive, change lead, architect, product lead or external consultant.
4. Current mission, vision and strategy: do they exist, and are they understood?
5. Customers: who are they, and what evidence exists about their journeys?
6. Change in flight: how many initiatives are running, and how are they governed?
7. Data available: financials, KPIs, customer data, process and system inventories.
8. Constraints: budget, timeline, regulation, politics.

## 2. Path chooser
Score each statement from 0 to 3 (0 = not true, 3 = very true). The path with the highest total is the entry point. Also look at the second-highest.

**Path 1: Business Design for Disruption**
- A new entrant or non-competitor is taking customers or margin.
- Our core revenue model is flat or declining.
- We are launching a new business, product line or venture.
- Leaders disagree on the strategy, or the mission and vision are unclear.
- We have no explicit game plan against competitors and disrupters.

**Path 2: Transforming Operating Models with Service Design**
- Customer satisfaction, retention or NPS is falling, or lags competitors.
- Customers complain about effort, delays or inconsistency across channels.
- We have a strategy but can't say which services and capabilities deliver it.
- The operating model was built around products or functions, not around journeys.
- We plan to restructure but haven't redesigned the processes, data or systems.

**Path 3: Managing the Transformation Journey**
- Many projects are running; few deliver their benefits.
- Projects are siloed, duplicated or not linked to objectives.
- The roadmap is either rigid, or it doesn't exist.
- Accountability for outcomes is unclear.
- Transformation budgets are growing but the ROI can't be shown.

Rules of thumb:
- A **start-up** runs 1 → 2 → 3.
- If Path 1 has just been done, run **Path 2 before Path 3**.
- If Path 3 scores highest but there is no target operating model, run a **light Path 2** first.

## 3. Failure-symptom scan (mapped to root causes)
| Symptom | Likely root cause | Model to work on |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong product or solution | Ideas not validated with customers | Business, Value |
| No traction with customers | Journey gaps; value not visible to customers | Value |
| Lack of internal cohesion | No shared mission or common language | Mission |
| Poor resource allocation and planning | Disconnected projects; no roadmap | Transformation |
| No funding, or money running out | Weak business model or unit economics; premature scaling | Business |
| Poor leadership and governance | Unclear accountability; hero culture | Mission, Transformation |

## 4. Transformation-culture check
Rate each statement from 1 to 5 (5 = strongly agree):
1. People speak up when they think something is wrong.
2. Ideas are tested with customers or stakeholders before scaling.
3. Failure in contained experiments is treated as learning.
4. Teams work towards a shared mission rather than individual heroics.
5. People ask for help and share knowledge across silos.
6. People understand how their work creates value.
7. There is a common language or framework for strategy and change.
8. Leaders sponsor transformation visibly and stay involved.

Interpretation: 32–40 is a culture of learning; 20–31 is mixed; below 20 means poor transformation culture, so fix it before large investments. Use this as a conversation starter, not a validated psychometric instrument.

## 5. Strategy-and-architecture practice approach
Find out which approach the client uses today. The recommended one is the fifth.
| Approach | Typical risk | Move towards the recommended approach by |
|---|---|---|
| Random & sporadic | Changes made in silos, with no view of the impact on the rest of the business and no alignment | Linking to mission and objectives; sharing data and feedback |
| Led by technology, engineering or operations | Solutions looking for problems; innovation driven by hype | Co-creating with customers and business; tying solutions to strategic outcomes |
| Led by change management | Bureaucracy; on-time and on-budget with no flexibility; weak executive sponsorship | Balanced value measures; executive sponsorship |
| Led by business management | Sales bias; value propositions built in isolation; handing off to third parties | Co-creating end to end with engineering and operations; balanced measures |
| **Strategically driven top-down, executed through co-creation** (recommended) | — | Clear actions and outcomes for each stakeholder; teams empowered, funded and trained; good communication |

## 6. Strategic quick-win filter
A candidate counts as a **strategic** quick win only if the answer to all of these is yes:
- [ ] It moves at least one objective in the Mission Model Blueprint.
- [ ] It can be delivered in 3 months or less, with resources available today (100% commitment).
- [ ] It doesn't create debt: no new silos, unnecessary processes or duplicate data.
- [ ] It builds or reuses a capability that the target operating model needs.
- [ ] Its result can be measured on at least one value measure.
- [ ] Customers or users can co-create or validate it.

## 7. What-if-we-do-nothing test
For each of the six value measures, estimate the 12–36-month trajectory if nothing changes. Where the data exists, quantify it: revenue at risk, cost of customer switching, opportunity cost. This builds the case for change and sets the baseline for the "Account" step.
