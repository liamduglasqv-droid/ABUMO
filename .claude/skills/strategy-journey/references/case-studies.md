# Case studies and lessons

Short summaries of the book's examples, each with its lesson. Use them as analogies in client conversations. Facts reflect the book's account (2020). Check them before quoting figures in a client deliverable.

## Failures: large enterprises
| Case | What happened | Lesson → model |
|---|---|---|
| **Blockbuster** | It was the market leader (about 9,000 stores). It protected its retail model, bought Circuit City rather than Netflix (reportedly offered for about $50M), and ignored the digital customer journey: streaming, search and no late fees. It went bankrupt in 2010. | Transform the operating model around the customer's changing journey and time → Value, Operating |
| **Kodak** | It invested to protect film and printing, gave its digital-camera lead to Sony, and sold its pharma business while Fujifilm diversified. Customers moved to sharing photos rather than printing them. | Operating-model complacency; protecting a USP customers no longer value → Mission, Business |
| **SunEdison** | It went on a debt-funded acquisition spree to become the biggest clean-energy player. Deliveries failed and it had no operating-model efficiencies. It collapsed. | Growth bought too fast in too many directions; you can't buy a business model without transforming the operating model → Transformation |
| **Lego** (turnaround) | It over-diversified into clothing, media, theme parks and watches without the capabilities to support them, and was losing about $1M a day by 2004. CEO Knudstorp refocused on bricks and children, and built capabilities around the core mission. | Innovation without strategic direction is dangerous; refocus on the mission and build agility → Mission, Operating |

## Failures: SMEs and start-ups
| Case | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Sugru** | A crowdfunded product customers loved. It was sold for much less than it had raised, and investors lost about 90%. The business plan lacked detail on operating costs, burn and scaling. | Investors and founders must check *how* the operating model will scale, not only the growth projections |
| **WebVan** | An online grocer valued at about $1.2B. It spent about $1B on warehouses and vans, burning about $1.8M a day against about $0.5M in sales, before it had proved the model in one region. It went bankrupt in 2001. | Premature scaling; ignoring adoption-curve data (the customers were still early adopters) |
| **Tutorspree** | A tutoring marketplace that depended on one channel (SEO). A Google algorithm change cut its volume by about 80%, and it had no other game plan or brand equity. | One strategy is not a game plan; a transactional model with no relationship or brand assets |
| **Everpix** | A good AI photo app with loyal users. It overspent on engineering, consultants and staff and barely marketed the product, so revenue stayed low and it went broke. | Build the whole value chain (sales, distribution, service), not just the product |

## Successes and worked examples
| Case | Used for | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon** | Born from two data sets (internet growth rate + best online category: books); marketplace; Alexa; Amazon.com operating model | Customer-obsessed and data-driven; multi-sided platform; data from IoT touchpoints |
| **Amazon Alexa** | Voice customer journey, then a target Service Operating Model | A service designed around a new journey, with an app ecosystem (network effects) |
| **Apple vs Nokia** | iPhone as a gateway to a platform business model | Incumbents missed the move from product to platform because they weren't tracking the right data |
| **Apple global operations** | Transformation Roadmap example | Operations as a strategic capability; staged roadmap |
| **Tesla** | Mission Model example | A mission with strong pull (the transition to sustainable energy); a platform and an energy network |
| **Udacity** | Digital Business Blueprint example | An education business model built on digital assets and industry partnerships |
| **CBA (Commonwealth Bank of Australia) home buyer** | Customer Journey Map example | Serving the whole journey (house hunting → mortgage → moving), not just the product |
| **IBM Business Solutions** | Service economy | A move from hardware to less price-sensitive solutions (it bought PwC Consulting in 2002) |
| **Grab vs Uber (SE Asia)** | Customer segments and localisation | Local journey fit and a super-app ecosystem beat a global incumbent |
| **Netflix** | Sticky effects | Personalisation and time saved made the service sticky and disrupted Blockbuster |
| **Facebook** | Network effects | Open API platform, the "Like" button (social sharing), and Groups (community co-creation) |
| **Skype** | Big Bang Disruption | Free internet telephony and video changed behaviour across industries |
| **Tony Robbins** | Proposition ecosystem | Layered propositions (events, books, coaching, community) around one mission |
| **Vitality** | Data-driven insurance | Wearables and data turn insurance into a health-engagement service |
| **Singapore Smart Nation** | Data-driven networks | Transformation across a whole ecosystem, city-wide |
| **DBS Digibank** | Business agility in banking | A digital-first bank built with agile, data-driven capabilities |
| **Harvard** | Data-driven enterprise | An incumbent that stays the leader by using data and its network assets |
| **Google vs Yahoo** | Focus and quick wins | Focus on a core service with data advantages beats a portal that does everything |
