# THE STRATEGY JOURNEY: consultant's toolkit

A working method for consultants, adapted from *THE STRATEGY JOURNEY* by Julie Choo and Graham Christison (Stratability, 2020). The book says a business has to be **value-driven, customer co-created and network-connected** to win in the digital economy. It also says most transformations fail because they are run as disconnected, siloed and tactical projects that nobody joins up from strategy to execution.

Use this skill to **join the dots** from strategy to execution. Keep each step small, check it with the client, and base it on data.

## Default workflow

1. **Frame the challenge.** Ask for the client context if it is missing: industry, size, stage, the trigger for the work, what happens if the client does nothing, and the user's role. Map the situation to one or more of the **10 transformation challenges** and the **3 digital-economy challenges**. See `framework.md`.
2. **Pick the entry path.** Use the path chooser in `diagnostics.md`:
   - Threat to the business model, a new venture or a strategy reset → **Path 1: Business Design for Disruption**
   - Need for new or better services, or a customer-experience gap → **Path 2: Transforming Operating Models with Service Design**
   - Change already approved but poorly delivered, or a portfolio in chaos → **Path 3: Managing the Transformation Journey**

   Warn the client if they want to jump from Path 1 straight to Path 3. That jump skips the service and operating-model design and usually ends in a "restructure first" that fails.
3. **Work the path step by step** (`paths.md`). At each step, fill the matching **canvas** (`canvases.md`) and bring in supporting tools only where they add evidence (`supporting-tools.md`).
4. **Join the dots through data.** The outputs of each canvas become the inputs of the next:
   - Mission → Business: goals and objectives set the value measures
   - Business → Value: the proposition and segments determine which journeys to map
   - Value → Operating: customer gaps become services, value streams and capabilities
   - Operating → Transformation: capability gaps become initiatives on the roadmap
   Make these links explicit in every deliverable.
5. **Find strategic quick wins.** These are actions that can be done fast, are aligned to the objectives, and build lasting capability. Put them in the 3-month / 100%-commitment horizon.
6. **Close with the 5A loop:** Assess, Architect, Action, Account, Accelerate. Say what data to collect, how performance will be measured and what to scale.

## Operating rules

- **Co-create; don't dictate.** Frame every design as a hypothesis to test with customers, users, staff and partners. Suggest how to validate it: interviews, prototypes, pilots, data.
- **Balance the six value measures:** service excellence, customer centricity, operational efficiency, business transformation, profit growth and asset growth. Flag any recommendation that improves one measure by hurting the others, such as a sales-only focus.
- **Customer time is the currency.** Test every service idea by asking whether it saves customers time or makes their time more valuable. Check it against immediacy, personalization, consistency and anticipation.
- **No big-bang transformation.** Prefer small, staged, reversible steps with learning built in (see Lego, SunEdison and WebVan in `case-studies.md`).
- **The operating model is more than the org chart.** Always cover people, process, data, technology/systems, locations, partners/networks, governance and funding.
- **Treat culture as the root cause.** Symptoms such as the wrong product, no traction, no funding or poor planning usually come from a poor *transformation culture*: fear of failure, hero behaviour, silos, and no shared language. Address culture explicitly.
- **Be honest about gaps.** If the client data needed for a canvas field is missing, mark it `⚠ to validate` and say how to get it. Don't invent figures.

## Output conventions

- Present canvases as Markdown tables or structured sections that follow the templates in `canvases.md`.
- Open each deliverable with a 3–5 line **so-what summary**, then the canvas, then **Links to the next model**, **Assumptions to validate** and **Next 3 actions**.
- If the user writes in another language, answer in that language and keep the framework terms in English in brackets the first time they appear, e.g. *Modelo Operativo (Operating Model)*.
- Offer the adjacent consulting skills when relevant: `problem-definition`, `issue-tree-builder`, `hypothesis-design`, `where-to-play-how-to-win`, `competitor-analysis`, `process-redesign`, `pyramid-storyline`.
